# Competitive landscape and positioning notes

## Research takeaway

Regulatory-change monitoring, obligation-to-control mapping, gap analysis, remediation workflows, evidence traceability and human review are already established product capabilities. ReguLens must not claim that those broad functions are globally unique.

Examples identified in a preliminary web search include RegAhead RegWatch (regulatory monitoring and control remediation), RegMapper/Kolena-style obligation/control mapping, and related regulatory intelligence products. These examples are a directional landscape check, not a complete market survey or endorsement. Product capabilities and branding should be rechecked before public claims.

## Demonstration-level differentiation

The prototype's differentiating demo is the **controlled failure-and-recovery loop**:

- judges can change the input control/evidence state;
- inject owner unavailability and a transient tool failure;
- inspect the exact source clause, mapping terms, risk reasons, task owner and retry trace;
- execute a sandbox remediation through explicit steps;
- require synthetic evidence, owner attestation and a retest;
- see the closure gate keep ambiguous/unmapped obligations open;
- export the event and decision record.

This is a defensible combination to demonstrate, not a guarantee of global novelty. Do not say "first in the world" or "100% unique" without a proper prior-art search.

## Submission framing

Position it as a local-first, explainable **regulatory change-to-verified-remediation simulation**. Be explicit that the current build uses deterministic processing and synthetic data; do not claim a deployed LLM, regulator-feed integration, real bank connector, or production legal assurance.
